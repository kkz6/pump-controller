PUMP CONTROLLER — OPERATING CONTRACT AND BRING-UP PLAN
Revision: 2026-11-19 / engineering prototype

STATUS

This document specifies intended firmware behavior and commissioning checks.
Matter firmware, the local dashboard, control logic and calibration software
have not been implemented or tested. The circuit and PCB are a prototype;
schematic generation and CAD checks do not establish mains safety, EMC,
protection performance or compatibility with every starter.

Application assumed: one 220–240 VAC, single-phase, 1 HP pump, with an existing
DOL contactor and correctly selected motor overload protection. The board's
two series relay contacts control the contactor coil circuit. They do not
switch the motor power conductors. Confirm actual coil voltage, pickup VA,
holding VA and suppression before connecting it. Retain protective earth,
upstream disconnection, circuit protection and the motor overload device.

LOCAL CONNECTION AND APP BEHAVIOR

Use Matter over 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi on ESP32-C6-MINI-1U-H8. The same Wi-Fi link can
serve an authenticated local browser dashboard with voltage, current, real
power, power factor, energy, water band, sensor validity and fault history.
No additional local server is required by this proposed architecture. Place
the approved external antenna outside the metal starter enclosure using an
insulated mounting arrangement; keep its cable in the isolated low-voltage
compartment and observe the module manufacturer's antenna restrictions.

Matter uses local IP communication. Once commissioned, routine local control
must work with the internet/WAN disconnected while the Wi-Fi router, local
network and relevant controller remain powered. Setup, Apple account services,
remote access, updates and some voice functions may still require internet.
Do not describe this as operation without Wi-Fi or without a powered LAN.
Allow local IPv6 and discovery traffic; avoid client isolation between the
phone, HomePod and controller. Test WAN failure explicitly. [1, 2]

The existing HomePod mini or HomePod (2nd generation) can be the Apple Home
hub. Its Thread capability does not require this device to use Thread.
Apple's supported accessory categories and displayed attributes are limited;
native Home tiles for water quantity, volts, amps and watts are not guaranteed.
Implement and validate a supported on/off endpoint for basic control, and use
the local dashboard for detailed telemetry. Matter attribute availability does
not itself establish that Apple Home will display that attribute. [1]

The Aqara hub model is unspecified. A Matter bridge and a Matter controller
have different roles: a bridge exposing Aqara devices does not necessarily
accept this third-party device. Aqara lists compatible Matter controllers,
including M3, separately. If the installed hub supports the endpoint, it may
be added through Matter multi-admin; pump operation must not depend on Aqara
being present. Verify behavior with the actual model and firmware. [2, 3]

Proposed app name: "Pump enable". ON means an enabled request, subject to the
physical mode, arm latch, water level and every protective inhibit. In AUTO,
an ON request can legitimately wait with the motor stopped because the tank
is full. The dashboard must distinguish enabled request, commanded relays,
measured running state, mode and reason for inhibition. A relay command alone
must never be presented as proof that the motor is running.

OFF from any accepted local/app command cancels the enabled request and keeps
it cancelled until a fresh ON/START. AUTO must not undo a user OFF on its next
scan. App ON never overrides the physical OFF selector, STOP, open high-high
loop, latched fault or the need for physical re-arm.

PHYSICAL CONTROLS AND RE-ARM

OFF: selector opens both MAN and AUTO returns. K1 coil power is interrupted,
and the hardware interlock clears the arm latch. Software commands both
relays off and clears the enabled request. Do not rely on software to detect
OFF before the coil is released.

MAN: a fresh local START arms the healthy controller and requests running.
Manual operation still obeys STOP, high-high, destination-full stop, source
availability, dry-run, metering/sensor validity and electrical protections.
MAN is not a bypass of the protective contactor-coil chain.

AUTO: a fresh local START arms the healthy controller and enables normal tank
filling. Start below the commissioned low threshold and stop at the high
threshold. Normal full-tank stops may retain the arm latch and enabled request
for the next fill cycle; a protective trip may not.

STOP is a normally closed contact in the 5 V coil-permission circuit. Opening
it removes K1 coil power and clears the arm latch. Closing STOP, changing the
selector back from OFF, or reclosing a high-high float must not restart the
pump. The next START must be a new press after the input has been released.
A button held through boot or a fault is not a new START.

RESET acknowledges a latched fault only after its cause is gone and the
required healthy observations have completed. RESET does not start or re-arm.
A separate, fresh local START is required. A power cycle is not a fault reset
or an instruction to resume. Network restoration and firmware reboot also
leave the pump off and unarmed. Automatic dry-run retries default to zero.

The SN74LVC1G74 arm latch is clocked by a deliberate ARM_CMD pulse after a
valid START; HEARTBEAT must never clock it. Supervisor, watchdog, MCU reset
and physical interlock clear permission. The MCU must also drop PERMIT_D and
RUN_CMD on every software fault, clear the run request, and never pulse ARM_CMD
merely because the loop starts working again. Heartbeat may advance only after
a complete healthy control cycle; a free-running interrupt must not conceal
a stalled control/sensor task. Use firmware brownout reset as another layer,
and test the complete rail-collapse response with the chosen supervisor.

The proposed default requires physical START after a hardware/protective
trip. Once locally armed, ordinary app OFF/ON can stop and request operation
without clearing a healthy arm latch. A deliberate mode transition between
MAN and AUTO should cancel the request and require START again.

PROBES, WATER DISPLAY AND AUTOMATIC FILLING

The existing five tank wires are assumed to be COM plus four electrodes
L1, L2, L3, L4 in ascending height. COM must remain immersed below L1.
Confirm those identities by measurement before connection; five wires alone
do not prove this arrangement. J21 inlet sensing requires an inlet electrode
AND a valid nearby inlet-common electrode/return. A single existing inlet
wire is insufficient until its return is identified. Never use mains neutral,
protective earth, a motor body or an unverified pipe as the probe return.

For bits ordered L1,L2,L3,L4, the valid settled wet patterns are:
  0000  below L1
  1000  between L1 and L2
  1100  between L2 and L3
  1110  between L3 and L4
  1111  at or above L4
Other patterns, uncertain amplitudes, stale samples and failed excitation
self-checks require an explicit unknown/fault display, not a fabricated level.
Probe transitions need bounded debounce to reject splashes; the independent
high-high float and STOP must not wait for that debounce.

Four threshold probes give five bands, not continuous depth. Calibrate their
heights against tank geometry before showing volume intervals. Do not label
them exact 0/25/50/75/100% or exact litres without that mapping. Below L1 is not
necessarily empty, and reaching L4 does not establish the tank's total volume.
The OLED should show the current valid band, measured running state, mode and
fault status. Rotate readable pages for level/state and V/A/W/PF values;
faults take priority over page rotation. Show "unknown" for invalid data.
Page content, order, brightness and timing are firmware/configuration choices.
There are no separate level/status LEDs in the selected design.

The 128 x 32 OLED shares I2C with essential probe/control peripherals. A whole
512-byte screen transfer takes roughly 12–13 ms at 400 kHz and must not block
the 2 ms probe sampling slots. Queue at most 16 display-data bytes per short
transaction (about 0.405 ms wire time including address/control bytes), admit
it only when the sensor scheduler has adequate slack, and yield between chunks.
Refresh values at 1–2 Hz; split command sequences as well as pixel writes.
Rendering reads a state snapshot and has no authority to write motor outputs.
An OLED-only NACK may disable the display while valid control continues; a
stuck shared bus or stale essential data must cause a latched protective stop.
Bus operations require deadlines; if the control loop stalls, the hardware
watchdog must remove permission. See oled-selection.txt for harness and tests.

Simple conductivity probes cannot distinguish every broken wire from dry
water, or every short/fouled probe from a wet probe. Conductivity depends on
water and cable conditions. Use the independent normally closed high-high
float to interrupt permission where overflow consequences require it. A
shorting link in J31 is only a bypass for controlled bench work and provides
no independent high-high protection. A wet inlet probe indicates water at
its location; it does not prove pump flow or successful priming.

The proposed excitation is balanced 25 Hz AC. Follow probe-front-end.txt for
phase-locked sampling, mux settling, fresh conversions and bias/excitation
checks. Do not run protective decisions from cached ADC or meter values.

DRY-RUN AND ELECTRICAL COMMISSIONING

Use calibrated real input power as the principal underload signal, with
current and power factor available for diagnosis. A universal "1 HP dry-run
current" is invalid: load depends on the actual motor, voltage, pump curve,
head, valve setting and water condition. Low real power can also indicate
closed-valve operation, loss of prime or a broken coupling. The software must
report an underload/dry-run fault without claiming it uniquely identifies the
physical cause.

Calibrate RMS voltage, RMS current, phase and active power against suitable
reference instruments at representative loads. Record normal wet operating
power across the intended head and supply range, including the lowest normal
power case. Select an underload threshold below all validated normal cases
and above the validated fault cases with allowance for measurement error.
If those ranges overlap, power alone cannot reliably protect that installation;
add an independent suitable flow/pressure input in a revised design.

Do not deliberately run a pump dry merely to obtain a calibration number.
Use manufacturer-approved test conditions, a controlled test arrangement or
validated reference traces. Inrush blanking and priming grace apply only to
the underload algorithm. STOP, high-high, invalid data and independent motor
overload protection remain effective during startup. Limit any priming grace
to the pump manufacturer's permitted condition and time.

PROVISIONAL COMMISSIONING SETTINGS — NOT VALIDATED PROTECTION VALUES

These values are engineering starting points for bench tests. Store final
settings with a version, range checking and calibration record. AUTO and
unattended MAN operation remain disabled until the installation is validated.

  Control cycle:              50 ms target; bounded deadline.
  Watchdog heartbeat:         every 250 ms after a completed healthy cycle.
  Probe sample averaging:     5 excitation cycles, approximately 200 ms.
  Excitation startup discard: at least 1 s, followed by valid self-checks.
  Probe wet/dry amplitudes:   >=100 mV / <=60 mV provisional; middle uncertain.
  Level transition debounce: 2 s maximum before stable classification.
  Inlet-dry debounce:         1 s provisional; then latched stop.
  Stale probe/meter data:     >1 s since a required valid result: latched stop.
  AUTO start:                L1 dry for 2 s, with all other conditions healthy.
  Normal full stop:           L4 wet for 2 s; independent HH acts immediately.
  Minimum off interval:      30 s provisional; increase to maker's limit.
  Minimum run interval:      none may postpone a stop or protective trip.
  Underload startup blank:   1 s provisional for inrush, within total grace.
  Total priming grace:       5 s provisional from the start command; shorten
                             if required by the pump manufacturer.
  Sustained underload time:  3 s after grace, subject to allowed damage time.
  Initial threshold study:   0.65 x measured normal wet power is a trial
                             candidate only; do not enable without validation.
  Automatic fault retries:  0; RESET then fresh START after fault clearance.

Set a maximum continuous fill time from measured worst-case legitimate fill
time and pump duty limits. A provisional study value is 1.5 times the longest
validated fill time, capped by permitted motor duty; an unset limit must
prevent unattended AUTO rather than mean unlimited run. Set voltage and
overcurrent thresholds from motor nameplate/manufacturer limits and measured
startup behavior, not generic values inferred from 1 HP. Existing thermal
overload protection remains required.

Detect failure to establish running power and power persisting after a stop.
Determine their time limits from measured contactor and motor behavior. If
current persists after both relays are commanded off, latch a fault and show
"motor still powered"; the board cannot guarantee interruption of a welded
external contactor. Use the upstream disconnect before investigation.

ORDERED BRING-UP AND ACCEPTANCE CHECKS

1. Unpowered inspection. Check every actual component against its datasheet,
   including supervisor pinout, relay coil voltage, connectors, isolation
   barrier, slots, spacing, mounting hardware and field-wire separation.
   Check the assembled board for shorts and wrong resistor values. Verify
   upstream fuse coordination for the installation's prospective fault
   current; the on-board fuse rating alone does not establish board SCCR.

2. Isolated low-voltage supply only. Keep all mains terminals and contactor
   wiring disconnected. Feed +5 V/GND through a current-limited bench supply
   with the AC/DC source unpowered and an approved isolation arrangement.
   Start at a low current limit and inspect consumption, 3.3 V and heating.
   The permanent 33 ohm PSU bleeder draws about 152 mA at 5 V by itself.
   Check buck startup and ripple before inserting expensive modules if
   assembly permits. Never back-feed a powered AC/DC module from USB/debug.

3. Supply and reset tests. With dummy loads, sweep the allowed 5 V range,
   apply MCU/Wi-Fi load steps, and scope +3V3, SAFE_CLEAR_N, ARM_Q, PERMIT_Q
   and both coil drives. Test slow ramps, fast dips, power interruption and
   recovery. Verify zero unintended pickup and no automatic re-arm. Test
   the new supervisor threshold and release delay, finite reset propagation,
   watchdog timing and actual relay pickup/release at temperature. The
   nominal voltage/tolerance calculation does not replace those tests.

4. Hardware stop chain without a motor. Use actual low-voltage selector,
   STOP and high-high float contacts. Test each individually while running
   dummy relay loads, then close it again and confirm a new START is needed.
   Hold START through boot and fault recovery. Force the MCU to reset, stall
   the control task and stop sensor updates while a timer still runs. None
   may leave K1 permitted beyond the documented protective deadline. Verify
   no external mains control wire is connected to J31/J32 or probe headers.

5. Probe simulator, then real water. Exercise every valid/invalid pattern,
   open/shorted wires, missing common, phase loss, ADC errors and long cable
   loading. Check amplitudes and electrode AC balance with suitable isolated
   instruments. Repeat with the installation's water and full cable length.
   Confirm inlet return and whether an independent HH float is installed.

6. Meter and application tests at low risk. Use a controlled isolated test
   setup to calibrate the metering chain and confirm CT orientation and
   burden. Never open the CT secondary while its primary carries current.
   Simulate calibrated underload/overcurrent/voltage faults before involving
   a pump. Verify every timeout, output state, RESET/START distinction,
   history timestamp and unknown-data indication.

7. Network acceptance with relay dummy loads. Pair with the actual Apple
   Home/HomePod combination and optional supported Aqara controller. Confirm
   ON/OFF meaning in MAN and AUTO. Disconnect WAN, then disconnect Wi-Fi,
   then power down hubs. Local physical controls and protective logic must
   continue; network loss must not fabricate telemetry or cause a restart.
   Check repeated/late commands, reboot, update and factory-reset paths.
   All firmware update and factory-reset operations first stop and unarm.

8. Qualified mains bench validation. Only after the earlier checks, use a
   qualified electrical test setup to validate insulation, protective earth,
   leakage, actual creepage/clearance, dielectric withstand and the required
   EMC/surge/EFT behavior under the applicable equipment requirements. Verify
   coil suppression and contact switching with the real DOL coil. Do not
   attach an earth-referenced oscilloscope or ordinary USB lead to the live
   meter domain. Follow an approved isolated measurement method.

9. Thermal/full-load check in the closed starter enclosure. Operate both
   relays, Wi-Fi transmit bursts, OLED worst-case pattern and metering. The
   conservative 5 V budget is 5.7 W including the minimum-load resistor,
   100 mA OLED engineering reservation and 25 mA at 3.3 V for J34; keep
   the AC/DC module local ambient at or below 50 degrees C. At 65 degrees C
   its 5 W derated output is insufficient for that budget. Verify the 33 ohm
   resistor's 0.677–0.844 W dissipation, required heat-spreading copper and
   temperatures of nearby probes, RF module, relay coils and capacitors.
   Keep the bleeder's heat away from measurement and RF parts. A closed box
   can be materially hotter than the surrounding room.

10. Supervised pump commissioning. Fit the correctly rated overload, verify
    rotation/flow/prime by approved means, measure wet running ranges, record
    fill times and validate limits. Confirm high and low electrode heights,
    HH float action, stop release and restart inhibition. Record the final
    settings and test results before enabling unattended operation. Fault
    injection must remain within the pump and test-equipment limits.
